# Area measurement standards — which are authoritative and which are not

Read this before writing a single area figure into a schedule. Getting the
*number* right is the easy half; being able to say **which definition it is and
who published it** is what stops the figure being argued away in a PAG meeting
or a lender review.

## The uncomfortable starting point

**There is no Australian Standard for measuring building area.** Standards
Australia has never published one, and no single method is mandatory. Every
definition below comes from a professional body, from statute, or from industry
usage — and they do not carry equal weight. Never write "GFA" on a page without
saying whose GFA.

## The five measures BDM reports

| Term | Defined by | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| **FECA · UCA · GFA** | AIQS — *Australian Cost Management Manual* (current edition 2022), Method of Measurement of Building Area | **Authoritative for cost planning.** AIQS is the professional body for quantity surveyors in Australia and the ACMM is the QS profession's standard reference. GFA = FECA + UCA is defined there. |
| **GBA — Gross Building Area** | Property Council of Australia, Australian Property Institute and Real Estate Institute of Australia — *Glossary of Property Terms* (joint publication) | **Authoritative.** Defined as *"the total enclosed and unenclosed area of the building at all building floor levels measured between the normal outside face of any enclosing walls, balustrades and supports"*. |
| **GFA (planning)** | The applicable state planning regulation, adopted by the local planning scheme (in Queensland: the Planning Regulation, adopted by the council's City Plan) | **Statutory.** This is the *only* GFA with legal force for plot ratio, density and infrastructure charges. |
| **NLA · GLA · GLAR** | Property Council of Australia — *Method of Measurement for Lettable Area: Commercial* (1997, reprinted 2008) | **Authoritative for lettable / leasable area in commercial property.** Covers tenancy areas only. |
| **IPMS 1 / IPMS 2** | International Property Measurement Standards Coalition | **International, voluntary in Australia.** The API is an IPMSC member and treats following IPMS as competent professional practice, but has not made it mandatory. |

## The trap that catches people on GBA

GBA is **not** in the PCA *Method of Measurement for Lettable Area*. That
publication covers tenancy areas (NLA / GLA) only. People cite it for GBA
constantly and it is wrong. The correct citation is the **PCA / API / REIA
*Glossary of Property Terms***.

If a Summary tab reports GBA without naming that source, it is incomplete —
even where GFA already carries its AIQS reference.

## Definitions as they must appear in the workbook

**FECA — Fully Enclosed Covered Area.** All enclosed covered floor area at
every level, measured from the **inside face** of the external walls. INCLUDES
basements, lift shafts, stairs, plant and equipment rooms, and internal
partitions. EXCLUDES open courts and light wells.

**UCA — Unenclosed Covered Area.** Roofed but not enclosed areas — balconies,
verandahs, porches, undercrofts, unenclosed access galleries — measured to the
**inside face of the balustrade**, excluding balustrade thickness. A balcony
that is not roofed is not UCA.

**GFA — Gross Floor Area.** GFA = FECA + UCA. The cost-planning measure. It is
NOT the planning definition and must not be used to test plot ratio.

**GBA — Gross Building Area.** The total enclosed and unenclosed area at every
level, measured between the **outside face** of enclosing walls, balustrades
and supports. The largest of the measures — it includes unroofed balconies and
the external wall thickness.

**Planning-scheme GFA.** Shown for comparison. Typically measured from outside
the external walls, excluding parking / loading / manoeuvring, building services
and plant, access between levels, ground floor public lobby, and all unenclosed
private balconies whether roofed or not. **Check the wording in the scheme that
applies** — it varies council to council.

**NSA — Net Sellable Area.** The saleable area of each apartment lot: internal
area to the inside face of external walls and the centre of party walls, plus
balcony / terrace to the inside face of the balustrade. This is the lot-boundary
convention for a community titles scheme and is the area revenue is calculated
from. NSA is not a published standard — it is a market convention, and it should
be labelled as such.

## Which number to hand over, and for what

| Question being asked | Measure to give | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "What's the $/m² rate?" | **GBA** | The all-in figure including basement, plant and balconies — matches how builders rate a job. |
| "Does it comply with plot ratio?" | **Planning-scheme GFA** | The only one with legal force. |
| "What's the cost plan built on?" | **GFA (AIQS)** = FECA + UCA | The QS profession's cost-planning measure. |
| "What's the revenue?" | **NSA** | The lot-boundary area that gets sold. |
| "How efficient is the building?" | **NSA ÷ GBA** | Saleable efficiency. Expect roughly 60–70% for apartments over basement parking. |

## Sanity ranges

- **NSA as % of GBA** — 60–70% typical for apartments over a basement car park.
- **Basement GBA per car bay** — 28–35 m²/bay including aisles and ramp.
- **GBA > GFA > planning GFA** — always, in that order. If not, something is
  mis-classified.
- **External wall band** ≈ perimeter × wall thickness. Cross-check the area
  difference between GBA and FECA against that product.
